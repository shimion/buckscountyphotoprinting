Giclée vs. photo prints

What are Giclée Prints vs Regular Photo Prints?

If you’re looking for prints that endure, whether for a gallery exhibition or to decorate your home, choosing between different printing methods can be overwhelming, especially if you’re new to printing. Let’s delve into the basics of giclée prints versus photo prints to help you decide which method best suits your needs.

Giclée Printing at Bucks Photo: Where Mastery, Technology, and Longevity Converge

At Bucks Photo, we specialize in giclée printing—a sophisticated, high-resolution printing technique designed for the precise reproduction of fine art and photography. Renowned for its exceptional detail, color accuracy, and archival integrity, giclée printing is the gold standard for artists and photographers who demand museum-quality results that stand the test of time. As a premier provider of giclée services, we pair industry-leading technology with top-tier materials to create prints that are as visually stunning as they are enduring.

The term giclée (pronounced zhee-clay) is derived from the French word le gicleur, meaning “nozzle,” and gicler, which means “to spray.” The name reflects the process itself—ultra-fine droplets of pigment-based ink are sprayed with remarkable precision onto archival-grade paper or canvas. Unlike traditional printers, giclée systems use up to 12 pigment-based inks, providing an extraordinary color range and subtle tonal transitions that faithfully replicate the original artwork.

Overseen by a master printer

What truly sets Bucks Photo apart is not only our advanced technology, but the expertise behind each print. Every giclée is meticulously overseen by our master printer, whose years of experience and finely trained eye ensure that every element—from color profiling to media choice—is handled with the utmost care. This level of craftsmanship elevates the process into a true fine art discipline.

Museum-grade papers and canvas

We offer a carefully curated selection of museum-grade, archival-quality papers and canvas options, including 100% cotton rag, textured watercolor paper, smooth matte finishes, and heavyweight artist canvas. Paired with our lightfast, pigment-based inks, these materials are engineered to last—producing prints with an archival lifespan of up to 200 years under proper storage or display conditions.

For artists, photographers and creatives

Whether you’re an artist reproducing limited editions, a photographer preparing for an exhibition, or a creative professional looking to preserve your work or sell it in its finest form, Bucks Photo delivers the precision, artistry, and longevity your work deserves.

The other way

Traditional Photo Printing

Photo prints, in contrast, are mass-produced using automated processes that reproduce images on chemically sensitized paper, rather than employing true archival-quality materials or methods. Unlike handmade or custom archival prints, which often involve meticulous control over materials and technique, photo prints are typically created by machines—either traditional photographic printers or digital exposure units—that expose the paper to a photographic negative or a digital image file. The image is then developed through a standardized series of chemical baths, a method rooted in the long-standing practices of darkroom photography.

While this process has historically been favored by photographers for its ability to capture tonal quality and natural color gradation, especially in portrait and landscape work, it comes with significant limitations and drawbacks. These prints are commonly made on inexpensive glossy or semi-gloss paper, which can enhance vibrancy and provide limited scratch resistance but also introduces glare that impairs visibility under direct light. Additionally, the emulsion layer on the surface is prone to chipping, especially around the edges, where handling or wear may cause flaking and visible surface damage.

Because these prints are not crafted with archival considerations in mind, they are especially vulnerable to environmental degradation. Over time, chemical photo prints will fade, yellow, or develop stains when exposed to light, moisture, or suboptimal storage conditions. As a result, despite their visual appeal at the time of printing, machine-made chemical prints are generally regarded as lower in both quality and longevity when compared to pigment-based or archival inkjet prints designed specifically for preservation.

Giclée and Traditional Photo Prints, the Difference

A key difference between giclée prints and traditional photo prints lies in their quality, longevity, and intended purpose. Giclée prints are produced using high-resolution inkjet printers that employ specialized pigment-based inks and fine, archival-grade materials. These printers often utilize a 12-color ink system, allowing for an exceptionally broad color gamut and the ability to capture intricate details, subtle tonal transitions, and rich, vibrant hues with impressive precision. Combined with acid-free, museum-quality papers or canvas, giclée prints are designed to meet the highest standards of fine art reproduction, ensuring that colors remain stable and true for decades without fading or yellowing.

Giclée prints

  • High-resolution inkjet with 12 pigment-based inks
  • Acid-free, museum-quality paper or canvas
  • Broad color gamut and subtle tonal transitions
  • Colors stay stable and true for decades
  • Overseen by a master printer

Traditional photo prints

  • Machine-made with a series of chemical baths
  • Low-cost glossy paper stock that causes glare
  • More limited, dye-based color range
  • Emulsion prone to chipping at the edges
  • Fades, yellows or stains over time

In stark contrast, chemically produced photo prints are typically machine-made using mass-production processes that prioritize speed and cost-efficiency over archival integrity. These prints are developed on light-sensitive photographic paper using a series of chemical baths and are often printed on low-cost, glossy paper stock that may look appealing initially but lacks long-term stability. The color range is more limited due to the constraints of the dye-based development process, and details may appear less refined when compared to the crisp resolution of a giclée print. Moreover, photo prints are prone to various forms of deterioration—including chipping, fading, and surface discoloration—particularly when exposed to light, humidity, or improper storage.

While photo prints can be suitable for casual or everyday display, they fall short of the durability and visual fidelity offered by giclée prints. For artists, photographers, and collectors seeking a premium, long-lasting presentation of their work, fine art giclée printing is the superior choice—offering a combination of craftsmanship, archival materials, and image quality that inexpensive chemical photo prints simply cannot match.

A note on “low-price” labs

Just to be fair and informative, when comparing our pricing to places like MPIX or other similar “low” pricing and “all-in-one” print, mat and frame shops… be warned, they do NOT use archival ink systems. The lower price prints use dye inks and chemical solvents which are NOT certified archival by the Fine Art Trade Guild. We only use pigment based ink systems for a true certified archival quality art print tested by the Fine Art Trade Guild.
